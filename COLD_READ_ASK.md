# Asking someone to read this cold

A draft you can paste and cut. The point is to make saying yes cheap and saying no easy.

---

**Subject:** 40 minutes of your judgement on an AI incident register?

Hi [name],

I have built a public register of twelve documented AI agent failures. Each one is
traced to the control that was missing, a test that would have caught it before launch,
and a signal that would have shown it after. It is at [URL].

I am looking for one person to read it cold and tell me where it is wrong. Not a
line edit. The things I cannot see myself:

1. **Is the method honest?** The Method page is the thing I would most like judged. It
   carries the inclusion rule, the coding rubric, the evidence grades and the limits.
2. **Does the headline survive you?** It is that in 5 of 11 cases the agent said nothing
   untrue and harm happened anyway, and that in none of them was an untrue statement
   sufficient on its own. I have already had to weaken it once.
3. **Where would a practitioner stop trusting it?** You know what a register looks like
   when someone has not run one.

What I am not asking for: proofreading, design opinions, or agreement.

**Time:** about 40 minutes. The Method page is 10 of those and it is the part that
matters.

**Credit:** you would be named on the page as the reviewer, with the date, if you want
that. If you would rather not be named, say so and you will not be. Either way the
version number moves to 1.0 only when somebody outside has read it.

**Deadline:** none. If you are not the right person or have no time, a one line no is a
completely good answer and I will not follow up.

Thanks,
Sana

---

## Who to ask

In rough order of usefulness:

- Someone who has run incident response or post-incident review in a regulated setting.
  They will know immediately whether the aftermath fields are realistic.
- An AI governance practitioner who has had to argue a control was effective to somebody
  external.
- A risk or audit professional from outside AI. The register's claims are ordinary
  evidence claims and they will not be distracted by the subject matter.

One is enough. Two is better than one only if the second is from a different discipline.

## What to do with what comes back

Every substantive point goes in the change log with the date, whether or not it is
acted on. A reviewer who sees their point recorded and declined is better served than
one who sees it silently dropped.
